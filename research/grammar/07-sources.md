All URLs verified on **2026-08-12**. Where a figure is quoted, the source it came from is named, and where a claim rests on a secondary summary rather than the primary text, that is stated.

**A general caveat on this strand.** Much of the underlying literature sits behind paywalls (Wiley, Cambridge Core, SAGE). Several claims below were read from **abstracts, ERIC records, publisher summaries and search-result summaries** rather than from the full papers. That is a weaker evidential basis than the dictionary strand had, where primary vendor documentation was directly readable. Claims sourced this way are marked **[abstract-level]**. ## Instructed SLA — effectiveness of grammar instruction

- [**Norris, J. M. & Ortega, L. (2000). Effectiveness of L2 Instruction: A Research Synthesis and Quantitative Meta-analysis.** *Language Learning*, 50(3), 417–528](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0023-8333.00136) — **[abstract-level]** Source for: focused L2 instruction produces large target-oriented gains; explicit instruction outperforms implicit; focus-on-form and focus-on-forms produce equivalent large effects; gains largest when metalinguistic explanation is included; synthesis window 1980–1998. Also at [ERIC EJ611436](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ611436).
- [Norris & Ortega (2001). Does Type of Instruction Make a Difference? Substantive Findings From a Meta-analytic Review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-1770.2001.tb00017.x) — companion paper.
- [Goo et al. — Norris & Ortega (2000) revisited and updated: Implicit and explicit instruction in L2 learning](https://benjamins.com/catalog/sibil.48.18goo) — the updated replication; **not read beyond the catalogue entry**. Worth reading in full before relying on the 2000 figures.
- [Explicit Approach — Foreign Language Teaching Methods, COERLL, UT Austin](https://coerll.utexas.edu/methods/modules/grammar/02/) — teaching-methods summary; used only for framing.

> **Unverified:** the widely-repeated claim that ~90% of Norris & Ortega's outcome measures were discrete-point tests. The measurement-bias critique itself is standard and acknowledged in the original; the specific proportion is quoted in [`01-evidence-base.md`](01-evidence-base.md) §1 as unverified and should not be cited as fact.

- [**Ellis et al. — Explicit and implicit knowledge and learning of an additional language: A research agenda.** *Language Teaching*, Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/explicit-and-implicit-knowledge-and-learning-of-an-additional-language-a-research-agenda/5AC1991F59C8059E9DD3F1141EE66E7D) — **[abstract-level]** Source for: the state of the interface debate, and the weak-interface position that explicit knowledge can fill gaps in implicit knowledge and increase its accuracy without converting directly.

## Skill acquisition theory, proceduralization, transfer-appropriate processing

- [**DeKeyser, R. M. & Suzuki, Y. (2025). Skill acquisition theory.** In VanPatten, Keating & Wulff (eds.), *Theories in Second Language Acquisition* (4th ed.), Routledge, pp. 157–182](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393631114_DeKeyser_R_M_Suzuki_Y_2025_Skill_acquisition_theory_In_B_VanPatten_G_D_Keating_S_Wulff_Eds_Theories_in_second_language_acquisition_An_introduction_4th_ed_pp_157-182_Routledge) — current statement of the theory.
- [DeKeyser — Automatization, Skill Acquisition, and Practice in Second Language Acquisition, *Encyclopedia of Applied Linguistics*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781405198431.wbeal0067) — **[abstract-level]** Source for: the declarative → procedural → automatized sequence, and the quoted prescription that *"a sequence of explicit rule learning, followed by a short period of activities focused on using explicit knowledge during performance of the target skills, and finally by a long period of repeated opportunities to use that knowledge, is likely to yield knowledge that is highly automatized."*
- [Contributions of declarative and procedural memory to accuracy and automatization during second language practice, *Bilingualism: Language and Cognition*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/bilingualism-language-and-cognition/article/abs/contributions-of-declarative-and-procedural-memory-to-accuracy-and-automatization-during-second-language-practice/60E10C774F6169657B86BF127CFAE9C1) — **[abstract-level]**
- [Question Type, Cognitive Load, and CEFR Alignment: Evaluating LLM-Generated EFL Grammar Drill Exercises (arXiv, 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.01592) — source for the transfer-appropriate-processing framing applied to grammar practice design, and for the state of LLM item generation. See also LLM generation below.

## Corrective feedback

- [**Li, S. (2010). The Effectiveness of Corrective Feedback in SLA: A Meta-Analysis.** *Language Learning*, 60(2), 309–365](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x) — **[abstract-level]** Source for: 33 primary studies (22 published, 11 dissertations); **medium overall effect d = 0.64**, maintained over time; implicit feedback better maintained than explicit; published studies no larger than dissertations; lab larger than classroom; shorter treatments larger than longer; foreign-language contexts larger than second-language contexts; 17 coded features, 14 used as moderators. Also at [ERIC EJ883422](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ883422) and [full text via Academia](https://www.academia.edu/2911659/Li_S_2010_The_effectiveness_of_corrective_feedback_in_SLA_A_meta_analysis_Language_Learning_60_309_365).
- [**Optimal timing of treatment for errors in second language learning — a systematic review of corrective feedback timing**, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9995700/) — **[summary-level]** Source for: of 20 studies, 10 favoured immediate feedback, 7 found no difference, 3 favoured delayed with internal-validity limitations. **The tallies come from a summary of this review, not from the review's own text — confirm before citing.**
- [The timing of corrective feedback in second language learning, *Language Teaching*, Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/timing-of-corrective-feedback-in-second-language-learning/0E8856852D0183E9DD91EDB4C249E245)
- [THE EFFECTS OF IMMEDIATE AND DELAYED CORRECTIVE FEEDBACK ON L2 DEVELOPMENT, *SSLA*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/effects-of-immediate-and-delayed-corrective-feedback-on-l2-development/B4B2D455749B752BD4F6DD636ACD688F)

## Spacing, retrieval, interleaving

- [**Kim, S. K. & Webb, S. (2022). The Effects of Spaced Practice on Second Language Learning: A Meta-Analysis.** *Language Learning*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12479) — **[abstract-level]** Source for: **48 experiments, 3,411 L2 learners**; medium-to-large effect for spaced over massed; no advantage for longer over shorter gaps on immediate tests but small effects on delayed tests; three spacing contrasts compared; **the authors' own note that most included studies were vocabulary and that more L2 grammar research is needed.** Also on [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358406370_The_Effects_of_Spaced_Practice_on_Second_Language_Learning_A_Meta-Analysis).
- [**Suzuki, Y. & DeKeyser, R. (2017). Effects of distributed practice on the proceduralization of morphology.** *Language Teaching Research*, 21(2)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1362168815617334) — **[abstract-level]** Source for the dissenting finding: massed practice produced accuracy equal to distributed practice and possibly **more rapid utterances**, in learners of Japanese L2 morphology. [Author's PDF](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Suzuki-DeKeyser-2017-LTR.pdf).
- [Suzuki, Y. (2018). The role of procedural learning ability in automatization of L2 morphology under different learning schedules, *SSLA* 40](https://yuichisuzuki.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Suzuki-2018-SSLA.pdf) — source for the short-spacing (3.3-day) vs long-spacing (7-day) conditions, n=60.
- [A Meta-Analytic Review of the Benefit of Spacing (PDF)](http://www.lscp.net/persons/ramus/docs/EPR20.pdf) — general (non-L2) spacing evidence.
- [The Synergistic Benefits of Systematic and Random Interleaving for Second Language Grammar Learning](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2211368119301329) — **[abstract-level]** Source for: systematic alternation during study + randomisation during practice produced a substantial interleaving benefit.
- [Suzuki, Yokosawa & Aline (2022). The role of working memory in blocked and interleaved grammar practice: Proceduralization of L2 syntax, *Language Teaching Research*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1362168820913985) — **[abstract-level]** Source for the accuracy/fluency trade-off: the blocked group used the structure with **greater fluency (fewer mid-clause pauses)**, the interleaving group **more accurately but at the expense of fluency**.
- [Interleaved practice enhances grammar skill learning for similar and dissimilar tenses in Romance languages](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475224001725)
- [The effects of interleaving and blocking practice on L2 contextualized grammar learning, *JSLS*](https://www.jbe-platform.com/content/journals/10.1075/jsls.00047.buh)
- [The effects of interleaved and blocked corpus-based practice on L2 pragmatic development, *SSLA*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/effects-of-interleaved-and-blocked-corpusbased-practice-on-l2-pragmatic-development/5A4444ED013A13A681076AF3E6F974CB) — **[abstract-level]** Source for: interleaved practice showed durable accuracy gains at a 5-week delayed post-test; blocked practice showed durable fluency gains.

## Input-based instruction

- [Processing Instruction: A Review of Issues](https://www.academia.edu/34582985/Processing_Instruction_A_Review_of_Issues) — **[summary-level]** Source for: the meta-analytic finding across **42 experiments in 33 published studies** that processing instruction beat production-based instruction for receptive knowledge, while production-based instruction was equally effective for productive knowledge. **The 42/33 figures come from a secondary summary — confirm against the meta-analysis itself.**
- [Processing Instruction and the Subjunctive (Northern Arizona University)](https://jan.ucc.nau.edu/jgc/research/pi/) — source for the replication finding of no PI advantage on the Spanish subjunctive.
- [The Effects of Input-based and Output-based Instruction on L2 Development, *TESL-EJ* 16(3)](https://tesl-ej.org/wordpress/issues/volume16/ej63/ej63a2/)
- [**Lee, S.-K. & Huang, H.-T. (2008). Visual Input Enhancement and Grammar Learning: A Meta-Analytic Review.** *SSLA*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/visual-input-enhancement-and-grammar-learning-a-metaanalytic-review/B9D0C50B09928C20C94548B37B29A042) — **[abstract-level]** Source for: **16 primary studies / 20 unique samples, d = 0.22**; enhancement "barely outperformed" unenhanced texts; the observation that visual input enhancement may slightly disrupt meaning processing.
- [Chung & Révész (2024) — Investigating the effect of textual enhancement in post-reading tasks on grammatical development (PDF)](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/119003/1/Chung&Revesz2024.pdf) — later work; source for the argument that single-shot enhancement studies may underestimate effects that need repeated exposure.

### Spaced repetition algorithms

The FSRS figures below come from **secondary sources — vendor and comparison blogs, not the open-spaced-repetition benchmark repository itself.** They are consistent across independent write-ups, which raises confidence somewhat, but the benchmark should be checked directly before any of these numbers is quoted as fact.

- [FSRS vs SM-2: why the modern spaced-repetition algorithm beats the classic (antiagent.io)](https://www.antiagent.io/blog/fsrs-vs-sm-2) — **[secondary]** Source for: FSRS fit to a public benchmark of **more than 700 million reviews**; more accurate recall prediction than SM-2 for **99.5% of users** even without per-user optimisation; roughly **20–30% fewer reviews** for the same retention; SM-2 published by Woźniak for SuperMemo in 1987.
- [Spaced Repetition Algorithms: From SM-2 to FSRS (Mindomax)](https://www.mindomax.com/spaced-repetition-algorithms) — **[secondary]** Source for: FSRS's three-variable memory model (difficulty, stability, retrievability) vs SM-2's single ease factor.
- [FSRS vs SM-2 (DeckStudy)](https://deckstudy.com/blog/fsrs-vs-sm2-modern-spaced-repetition) · [Neurako](https://www.neurako.com/blog/fsrs-vs-sm2-spaced-repetition-algorithms-compared) · [Kachika](https://kachika.app/en/blog/spaced-repetition-algorithms/) — **[secondary]** corroborating write-ups; source for Anki adopting FSRS as recommended default in late 2023.
