## 1. Before 1500 — the forest that was never wild
**Status: Verified**
- **Ferreira et al., 2026, *Nature***: Confirmed. The paper "Over 20,000 precolonial earthworks in the Southwest Amazonia" by Ferreira et al. was published in *Nature* in July 2026. It confirms the estimates of 1.25–3 million people in 100–300 AD in the area.
- ***Terra preta* coverage and geoglyphs**: The estimates (150,000 km²) and the 2017 PNAS study (450+ earthworks in Acre, 2,000–650 years BP) are consistent with the cited literature.

## 2. The colony that received the most enslaved people on earth
**Status: Verified**
- **Slave Voyages Database Statistics**: Confirmed. 12.5 million embarked, 10.7 million disembarked. 
- **Brazil's share**: Confirmed. Brazil received approx. 4.86 million (roughly 45%). The US received about 389,000 (roughly 4%).
- **Other details**: The estimated ~700,000 people enslaved at the time of the Lei Áurea in 1888 is historically accurate.

## 3. The prince who declared independence from his own father
**Status: Verified**
- **Timeline of the Court's flight**: Confirmed. The fleet left Lisbon on November 29, 1807, and arrived in Rio on March 7, 1808. The King stayed 13 years, returning in April 1821.
- **Numbers**: Confirmed. The estimate of 10,000–15,000 courtiers and officials is widely cited.
- **Timeline of Independence**: Confirmed. September 7, 1822 (Proclamation), October 12, 1822 (Pedro declared Emperor), and August 29, 1825 (Treaty of Friendship and Alliance).

## 4. Abolition, and who actually ended slavery
**Status: Verified**
- **Abolition Law**: Confirmed. The Lei Áurea was signed on May 13, 1888, freeing roughly 700,000 people.
- **Abolitionists**: Confirmed. Luiz Gama's background (illegally sold as a child), José do Patrocínio (pharmacist/journalist), and André Rebouças (engineer/professor) are accurate. Rebouças and Patrocínio's founding of the Abolitionist Confederation (in 1883) is also historically correct.
- **Movements**: Confirmed. Escapes around Santos and Campos, and the ongoing creation of quilombos leading up to 1888 are well documented in modern historiography.

## 5. Vargas and the invention of modern Brazil
**Status: Verified**
- **Timeline**: Confirmed. Vargas took power in 1930, declared the Estado Novo on November 10, 1937, which ended on October 29, 1945.
- **Key Events**: Confirmed. The CLT (Consolidação das Leis do Trabalho) was created in 1943. Vargas' suicide occurred on August 24, 1954.

## 6. Twenty-one years, and the deal that ended them
**Status: Verified**
- **AI-5 & Dictatorship**: Confirmed. AI-5 was enacted on December 13, 1968.
- **CNV Report**: Confirmed. Delivered in December 2014, citing 434 killed or disappeared.
- **Transition details**: Confirmed. The 1979 Amnesty Law shielded both sides. The *Diretas Já* rally on April 16, 1984 in São Paulo gathered around 1.5 million people. The Dante de Oliveira amendment failed by 22 votes. Tancredo Neves was indirectly elected on January 15, 1985 (480 to 180 votes) and died before taking office, passing the presidency to José Sarney.

**Conclusion:** All dates, figures, and major historical claims documented in the decisions file are accurate and reflect current academic consensus or official records. No changes have been made to the source file.
