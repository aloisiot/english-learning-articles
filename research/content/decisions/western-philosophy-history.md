# Western Philosophy — A History of Ideas — decisions

Progress tracking for this series: `research/content/progress/western-philosophy-history.md`

## Theme & throughline

A chronological chain through six thinkers, where each one's central move
is a direct, documented reaction to the one before — not a curated set of
"great philosophers" loosely grouped by era. Independent from "Eastern
Philosophy and the Self": different question, no shared subjects, no
mirrored structure (explicit user instruction, 2026-08-26).

Verified chain (each link checked against sources before being kept —
per explicit user instruction: "only create connections where there are
real connections between their ideas... keep it consistent with all
resources you find"):

1. **Socrates** cross-examines people's confident definitions of virtue
   (the elenchus), exposing the gap between claimed and real knowledge.
2. **Plato** turns that search for stable definitions into the Theory of
   Forms: eternal, unchanging archetypes behind the changing world. The
   Forms were "originally developed to answer Socrates' question of the
   validity of definitions."
3. **Aristotle** explicitly rejects the Forms (Third Man Argument,
   epistemological objections) as an unnecessary extra world, and builds
   an empirical alternative grounded in observation of things themselves.
4. **Descartes** *(~2000-year gap — see "Gap acknowledged" below)* breaks
   with the Aristotelian-Scholastic method he was taught (its reliance on
   sense-based "probable cognition"), replacing it with certainty built
   from the doubting/thinking self (cogito).
5. **Kant**, by his own account "woken from dogmatic slumber" by Hume's
   empiricist attack on Cartesian-style rationalism, builds a synthesis
   where the mind actively structures experience (synthetic a priori
   judgments).
6. **Nietzsche** directly attacks Kant's "thing in itself" as
   self-contradictory/incoherent, and replaces Kantian duty and reason
   with will to power — rejecting the whole metaphysical scaffolding
   (Forms, God, noumena) the previous five had been building in
   different ways.

## Gap acknowledged, not smoothed over

Aristotle → Descartes spans roughly 2000 years with no direct textual
link. The connection kept is specifically: Descartes reacting against the
*Scholastic* Aristotelian tradition he was taught at his Jesuit college —
not against Aristotle's own texts directly. Do not rewrite this into a
smoother, unbroken lineage in a future revision; the gap is real and the
mechanism of the link (Scholasticism, not Aristotle himself) matters.

  the table above — do not add connective tissue for narrative smoothness
  that isn't backed by a source.

  or per-article shape — this is a genuinely independent series.
